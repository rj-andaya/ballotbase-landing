Research & statistics: the evidence a bill turns on.

Beside the bill record, PolicySpan keeps the published research and the official statistics a policy argument rests on: government research and audit reports as full text with their tables, the scholarly literature by catalog, and official statistical series down to the county and the city. Every item carries its publisher, date, license and exact source, so a finding is a quotation from a named document or a figure from a named table, never a summary of a summary.

PolicySpan is not affiliated with or endorsed by any of the publishers or agencies named here. We name them because that is where the records live. Counts as of 2026-11-02; the corpus grows nightly.

Research is context published by others. It is never part of a bill's own record, and the product keeps the two apart: a document is attached to a bill as documented only when the document itself names the bill, as a Congressional Budget Office cost estimate or a Legislative Analyst's Office analysis does. Everything else is a topical match on the bill's subject and is labeled that way. For how direct sourcing shapes the rest of the product, see the Data & Sourcing overview.

Nonpartisan research and audit arms of the U.S. Congress and the California and Oregon state governments. Their reports are the written record legislators and committees work from, and most are public domain or public record.

Sourcing

Each publisher's own release channel: GAO's report feed and PDFs, the LAO and State Auditor report pages, the Oregon Audits Division's open records list, CBO's publication feed, and for CRS the EveryCRSReport archive of the official CRSReports.congress.gov releases. Tables are transcribed from the publisher's PDF cell for cell and checked against the page text; a table is marked verified only when every number in the grid was found on the page it came from.

Good to know

  • A finding is a quotation. Sections are the publisher's text under the publisher's headings, with the page number for PDF sources. Nothing is paraphrased in storage, and Polly reports findings as the document states them, with publisher, year and venue.
  • A table is the publisher's cells. Figures are quoted from verified tables only, and every table links to the PDF opened at that page. Nothing is computed, rounded or converted. Readers can report any table from the product; a report marks it unverified until checked.
  • Water figures are network roll-ups, not a census. A county value is the median across the wells or gages a network monitors, and the number of sites travels with every figure, as does the list of sites behind the latest value. Where a state network and the federal network both cover a county, both are shown and named. Nitrate figures describe monitoring, study and public-supply wells, not every private well.
  • Housing production is permits authorized, not units built. The unincorporated part of a county is its own row.
  • Survey estimates carry no margins of error yet. ACS and CHAS figures are estimates for a period and are labeled as such.
  • The literature is a sample, not a systematic review. It is chosen by evidence questions and searched by words; Polly says so when a question turns on it.
  • Not loaded yet: HUD point-in-time homelessness counts and the LIHTC property file; CBO's full publications (access check); Oregon's own city housing production counts.
